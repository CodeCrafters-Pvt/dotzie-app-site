A spending plan is a limit you set for yourself and a screen that tells you,
honestly, how you're doing against it.

## Step 1 — Pick a shape

**Records → Spending plan**, then create a plan. First you choose how it works:

| Mode            | What it means                                |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| **Overall cap** | One number. Spend under it.                  |
| **By category** | Limit each area of spending on its own.      |
| **Hybrid**      | A total ceiling, divided inside by category. |

Start with **Overall cap** if you've never budgeted in an app before. One number
you either stay under or you don't is far easier to keep up with than eleven
category limits you'll stop maintaining by week three. You can always move to
**Hybrid** once you know your own numbers.

## Step 2 — Set it up

- **Period** — Daily, Weekly, Monthly or Custom. Custom shows FROM and TO date
  cells.
- **Overall cap** (overall and hybrid modes) — enter the amount, and the app
  shows an "about X a day" hint so the number means something day to day.
- **Category limits** (category and hybrid modes) — add your categories and type
  a limit for each. An allocation bar shows how the split is going; in hybrid
  mode it also tells you how much is **unallocated**, or turns red with **X over
  cap** if your category limits add up to more than the ceiling.
- **Future expenses only** — optional. Counts only spending from the moment the
  plan starts, so a mid-month plan doesn't open already half-spent.
- **Warning thresholds** — choose when you want to hear about it: 50%, 75%, 90%
  or 100%.
- Optionally, save the whole setup as a **budget template** to reuse next period.

## Reading your plans

**Records → Spending plan** shows every plan with health counters:

- **Healthy** — comfortably within the limit.
- **Watch** — past a warning threshold.
- **Over** — past the limit.

Tap a plan for its full summary: overall progress, the per-category breakdown,
and how much is remaining or overspent.

The **Spending plan** card on your [dashboard](/guide/dashboard) shows the same
progress as a donut, if you'd rather see it every time you open the app.

## When it warns you

Budgets are what drive most of your [alerts](/guide/alerts) —
_approaching budget limit_, _budget exceeded_ and _budget period ended_ all land
in the Alerts tab. They arrive there whether or not push notifications are
switched on, so turning notifications off doesn't mean missing them.

> **Adding an expense outside your plan?** The transaction form warns you when a
> category isn't part of the active plan. That's a nudge to either recategorise
> the entry or widen the plan — not an error.
