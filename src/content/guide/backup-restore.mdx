## Moving to a new phone

If that's why you're here, the short version:

1. **On the old phone** — Settings → Backup & restore → tap the status card to
   back up now. Then **Manage backups → share a copy** and send the file
   somewhere you can reach from the new phone.
2. **On the new phone** — on the very first screen of the app, choose
   **Restore my data**, pick the file, enter your backup password.

That's it. Your name and starting balance come back pre-filled, and the app
reports how many records were restored.

> **This is the only copy of your data.** Dotzie has no servers and no account,
> so nothing is waiting in a cloud to be re-downloaded. If the phone is already
> gone and you have no backup file and password, the data cannot be recovered —
> by anyone, including us. That's the trade-off that makes it private.

## First-time setup — "Protect your backups"

**Settings → Backup & restore.** Two steps the first time:

### 1. Set a password

Backups are encrypted with it, and you can add an optional **hint**. In the
app's own words: _you can save and share backup files, so only you can open them
with this password._

### 2. Choose where backups go

- **Android** — pick a folder. Backups can't be saved until you do.
- **iOS** — fixed, at _Files › On My iPhone › Expense Tracker_.

When you finish, Dotzie writes your first backup immediately: _Backup is on.
Your first backup is saved, locked with your password._ (If there's nothing to
back up yet, it tells you that honestly instead of pretending.)

## Backing up

- The **status card** on the Backup & restore screen _is_ the back-up-now
  button. It shows backup health and when the last one ran, and fills as it
  works.
- **Backup frequency** — off, daily, weekly or monthly. Automatic backups **run
  when you open the app** — Dotzie isn't running in the background waiting, so
  if you don't open it for a week, it doesn't back up for a week.
- On Android, if you haven't picked a folder, the frequency row warns that it
  _won't run until you pick a backup folder below_.
- **Change backup password** — note carefully: **files saved before the change
  still need the old password.** Changing it doesn't re-encrypt old backups.
  Keep the old password until you've replaced those files.
- Backup and restore can't overlap. Rows disable themselves while one is
  running.

## Restoring

Reachable from the status card's **Restore** action, from Settings search
("restore"), or from the first screen of a fresh install.

1. **Pick a file** with the system file picker — or expand **choose from your
   saved backups** to pick one Dotzie already wrote. Saved backups show their
   date and password hint.
2. **Enter the backup's password.** It's verified _before_ anything is written,
   so a wrong password can't damage what you already have.
3. The restore runs full-screen with a progress indicator and reports how many
   records came back.

> The navigation bar hides during a restore. That's deliberate — a restore
> rewrites your data, and wandering off mid-write is exactly how data gets
> mangled. Let it finish.

## Managing saved backups

**Settings → Backup & restore → Manage backups** ("Your backups").

- Every saved copy, newest first, labelled by date and showing its hint. The
  newest is tagged **Most recent backup**.
- Tap a file for its actions: **restore it**, **share a copy**, or **remove
  it**.
- **Only the most recent few backups are kept** — older ones are replaced
  automatically. Copies you've saved elsewhere aren't in this list and aren't
  touched.
- **Share a fresh copy** exports everything up to _right now_ and sends it
  wherever you like. That's different from sharing one of the older saved files
  — use it when you're about to switch phones.

## A routine worth keeping

Set frequency to **weekly**, and once a month share a fresh copy somewhere off
the phone — your own cloud drive, a laptop, a USB stick. Backups sitting only on
the device they're backing up don't survive the thing you're protecting against.
